# D-20260801-03 — Scope and security questions are decision-material and proportional

Index: [DecisionHistory.md](../DecisionHistory.md)

## Detail

### Evidence and objective

The user confirmed the delivery-efficiency recorder baseline, then identified
that the universal policy had turned a routine invocation of one reviewed skill
or tool into an exhaustive threat-model and over-engineering questionnaire. The
question itself added delay and context even though the established posture was
being preserved.

The objective is to ask before consequential extra work without asking broad
hypothetical questions that cannot change the pending implementation.

### Selected behavior

- Use recorded decisions, requirements, confirmed assumptions, and read-only
  discovery before asking the user.
- Ask only when a concrete pending choice contains an unresolved answer whose
  wrong value could materially change outcome, scope, controls, cost,
  complexity, maintenance, reversibility, or risk and thereby cause meaningful
  additional work, over-engineering, or omission of a necessary control.
- Ask the smallest concise set of material questions and scale supporting detail
  to the impact of the choice. A full security baseline is appropriate only when
  the concrete decision actually depends on every baseline area.
- Routine execution of a reviewed skill or tool that preserves its documented
  scope and security posture uses the existing record and does not reopen it.
- Unknown assumptions material to a control still cannot justify that control.
  An unassessed area unrelated to the pending decision may remain explicitly
  unknown or out of scope.
- A material expansion still requires explicit approval before action, and an
  implemented security-posture decision still cites user-confirmed assumptions.

### Alternatives and boundaries

- The prior every-potential-expansion questionnaire was rejected because it
  spends time and context on choices that cannot affect the requested work.
- Removing the approval and assumption gates was rejected because consequential
  unrequested work and invented security premises would return.
- A fixed short questionnaire was rejected because even a short irrelevant
  interview is waste; the pending decision determines which questions matter.
- Agent-inferred answers remain prohibited. Proportionality narrows what must be
  asked, not who may supply a material project fact.

This decision supersedes only the blanket question thresholds in D-20260731-01
and D-20260731-02. Their remaining protections continue to apply.
