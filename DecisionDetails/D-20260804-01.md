# D-20260804-01 — UI completion requires exercised working interactions

Index: [DecisionHistory.md](../DecisionHistory.md)

## Detail

### Context

The universal policy required truthful controls and end-to-end behavior, but it did not require an
explicit completion-time inventory of the controls users can invoke. An agent could therefore pass
route, rendering, screenshot, visual-comparison, or geometry checks while leaving buttons, links,
menus, filters, forms, shortcuts, or clickable cards inert or only partially wired.

### Decision

- Every visible enabled control in the agreed UI performs its stated action through the rendered
  user-facing surface and produces the promised downstream result. Mere handler, route, component,
  toast, log, or local-state existence is insufficient for a navigation, persistence, integration,
  or other downstream promise.
- Before completion, one full evidence pass inventories every visible interaction on every included
  screen, state, and responsive variant, maps it to its journey and expected result, and exercises
  applicable success, cancellation, validation, permission-failure, recovery, and persistence paths.
  Non-critical gaps are collected through the full pass, then batch-fixed and rechecked.
- Each missing or partial behavior receives a specific active `CompletionLedger.md` entry naming its
  affected journeys, screens, variants, controls, files, behavior, impact, unblock condition, and
  rendered end-to-end verification. A generic future-production entry cannot conceal it.
- A future control appears only when the specification requires communicating future availability;
  it remains semantically disabled, non-actionable, visibly unavailable, and ledger-tracked, so the
  current delivery remains incomplete until implementation or explicit removal from agreed scope.
  Information about an out-of-scope future feature is noninteractive content, not a control.
- UI completion requires no behaviorless enabled controls, no requested journey without rendered
  end-to-end evidence, and no request-related completion-ledger item.
- Mock-data prototypes may use synthetic data, but every visible interaction works truthfully within
  the declared prototype boundary. This does not convert prototype behavior into a production claim.
- The inventory covers only agreed screens, journeys, states, and responsive variants. It neither
  expands agreed work nor invokes or authorizes a broader exhaustive audit.

### Alternatives rejected

- Visual or structural verification alone was rejected because it proves appearance or wiring shape,
  not the result of invoking the control.
- Spot-checking representative controls was rejected because repeated or secondary actions can remain
  inert while the sampled happy path works.
- Enabled placeholders were rejected because they communicate available behavior that does not exist.
- Hiding gaps in one generic future-production ledger entry was rejected because it loses the journey,
  control, impact, and verification needed to finish the agreed scope.

### Verification

The universal-policy checker requires the scoped interaction inventory, per-gap ledger detail,
completion-blocking future-control gate, rendered-interface evidence, and zero-gap completion
conditions. Its self-test includes must-catch fixtures for a missing contract, enabled placeholder,
optional or sampled inventory, generic ledger item, unlabelled future control, overbroad audit,
inert-complete claim, screenshot-only proof, and handler-only proof. Negative-phrasing controls guard
precision, alongside passing cases for a truthful synthetic prototype and an explicitly specified
disabled future control.
